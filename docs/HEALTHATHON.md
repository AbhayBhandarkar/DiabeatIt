# Healthathon 2026 India — demonstration and business case

## One-line pitch

DiabeatIT helps people managing diabetes and their families keep a clearer medication picture between appointments—without taking treatment decisions away from professionals.

## Three-minute demonstration

1. Start an isolated fictional demo. Introduce Meena (patient), Arjun (adult companion), and Kavya (fictional reviewer).
2. On Today, record a glucose observation and choose **I'm unsure** for a medicine. Explain that missing app activity is not proof of missed medication.
3. Open Medicines. Show the fixed Prescription Change Map: metformin appears in an existing prescription and a new combination-product description. Replacement intent is unknown. Nothing is automatically stopped or started.
4. Switch to the companion view. Show Care Handover and how the author/time of reports remain visible. Documents are not shared by default.
5. Switch to the fictional reviewer. Resolve the fixture with a clear **fictional** note; do not enter real treatment advice. Publish a new plan note. Only notes/version change, not medication instructions.
6. Switch back to the patient. Read back and acknowledge the note. Show how **I'm unsure** does not count as acknowledgement.
7. Open Appointment Ready; print/save the visit summary. Finish with food portions, one source-linked lesson, and light/dark phone layouts.

Always say **demonstration**, not live clinical integration. The reviewer is not a real consultation service. No CGM API, OCR, or clinical interaction engine is connected.

## Differentiation hypothesis

The product combines prescription-change visibility, uncertainty-aware recording, factual family handovers, and appointment continuity in an older-adult-oriented interface. These individual features are not claimed to be industry firsts. Competitive superiority and health outcomes have not been measured.

## Income strategy

| Offering | Proposed value | Payer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Essential | Core records, basic sharing and evidence-linked education | Free |
| Family Plus | Additional coordination, richer household tools, extended handover/report workflows | Family |
| Scoped review | Defined medication-reconciliation service by appropriately qualified staff | Family/clinic/sponsor |
| Clinic workspace | Staff assignment, onboarding and workflow integration | Clinic/care organization |

₹149–₹299/month is a **family pricing research hypothesis**, not validated willingness to pay or a promised price. Professional services need separate scope and cost limits; no unlimited clinical access promise.

Suggested launch channel: small clinic/pharmacy partnerships after appropriate approvals. Test activation, family retention, reviewer workload, cost-to-serve, conversion, and cancellations before forecasting revenue.

Cost model: net subscription receipts minus taxes/payment fees, cloud/storage, support, licensed medication data, acquisition, and allocated clinical time. A business can have high user engagement and still poor economics if reviewer time is unbounded.

Never paywall an already identified concern, essential urgent-help information, consent revocation, or basic data export. No health-data sale, health-targeted advertising, or paid medicine ranking.

## What judges may ask

**How do you prevent wrong recommendations?** We do not issue treatment recommendations. In this build, clinical screening is not connected; the demonstration highlights uncertainty and routes a fictional review.

**Does it work with every CGM?** No. Approved manufacturer and native platform integrations are future gated adapters, with device/region-specific validation.

**Is the nutrition score validated?** No. It is an expandable, manually curated research fixture. The food catalog is illustrative. Real deployment requires verified data and dietitian validation.

**Can it operate commercially?** The modular code, persistent backend, authorization tests, and documented interfaces provide an engineering starting point. Real identity, multi-tenant clinical operations, integrations, compliance and clinical validation remain work—not a finished commercial health service.

**Why would someone pay?** The hypothesis is reduced coordination effort and a clearer appointment routine. Willingness to pay must be measured, not assumed.

## Next pilot design

Recruit representative older adults, family companions, and pharmacists through an approved program. Test task completion, comprehension of uncertainty, shared-device identity errors, review time, and practical barriers. Do not claim reduced overdose or improved glucose outcomes without an appropriately designed study.
